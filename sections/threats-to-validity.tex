\section{Threats to Validity}

\textbf{Construct validity.}
Typed edges and explicit rules measure a selected structural view, not component responsibilities, rationale, runtime quality attributes, or developer usefulness. Reconstruction is model-conditioned: expected component identifiers and metadata help define the observed view. An omitted relationship can disappear from both incremental and full scans without an equivalence failure. File/line agreement is case-level and split between observed call sites and deterministic missing-relation anchors; it measures neither completeness/ranking of evidence nor reviewer comprehension. D2 specificity covers its declared hard negatives, not all unsupported syntax. Parsed-file counts are not CPU-work measurements.

\textbf{Internal validity.}
The authors developed both the analyzer and datasets. D1 topology intent preceded integration, but executable patches and exact locations co-evolved with the detector. P3 reuses those known patches. D2 was authored from v0.1 errors and guided v0.2, so tuning and confirmation risks remain. Isolated patches, raw predictions, integrity manifests and duplicate execution expose inconsistency but do not establish independent semantic labels. The call-site/anchor split and scope-specific RQ wording are post-hoc reporting clarifications; frozen labels and acceptance gates were not changed.

\textbf{External validity.}
One synthetic TypeScript/Node.js system with 20 patches and 40 detector signals cannot represent production repositories or longitudinal histories. Wrappers, dynamic endpoints, inter-file flows, shadowing, dependency injection, generated code, unconventional monorepos, other languages, IaC, and runtime-only relations remain unevaluated or incompletely observed. Hostname-level targets ignore port and path; AMQP broker paths do not prove routing compatibility. No industrial deployment or developer study was performed. Repository protection introduced later is not evidence of governance effectiveness in this evaluation.

\textbf{Comparative validity.}
No external comparison was executed within the reported evaluation, and no accepted comparator result or common label mapping is available. v0.1 versus v0.2 is within-tool regression only. Module imports and remote service calls are different units unless an independently reviewed mapping establishes a shared abstraction. Unsupported relationships must not become comparator false negatives. Tool versions, configurations, dependency resolution, common labels and scoring units must be frozen before interpreting comparative results.

\textbf{Literature-positioning validity.}
Related Work is a purposively scoped narrative synthesis, with source-selection and interpretation bias. It may omit relevant work; overlapping projects are not independent replications. These are limitations of the chosen narrative method, not temporary gaps awaiting completion of another review. Neither exhaustive coverage nor absence of competitors is established. The supplementary access register distinguishes metadata, available full texts, extracted evidence and pending human appraisal. Citation recency and DOI identity alone do not establish full-text verification or venue quality.

\textbf{Conclusion validity.}
The few changed graph items and targeted signals are finite regression objects, not independent random samples from a defined repository population. Accordingly, no population accuracy estimate, significance test or confidence interval is inferred from perfect agreement. Latency p50/p95 are descriptive nearest-rank statistics on one recorded environment, not a causal incremental-analysis effect or a scalability estimate.

\textbf{Reliability.}
Inputs, runtime archives and normalized outputs are pinned and hashed. Each current input is run twice; P3 checks cache miss/hit and full-scan agreement. The older detector result remains provenance-validated rather than continuously regenerated. Historical Windows and Linux functional replays support portability of the controlled protocol, while latency remains the original Windows recording. Unrecorded machine-load conditions and a trusted local graph-cache assumption limit reproducibility. Later software changes are separated from the revisions that produced these observations.
