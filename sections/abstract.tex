\begin{abstract}
Software teams can keep builds green while implementation relationships drift away from an approved architecture, leaving reviewers without a repeatable way to decide whether a change is harmless, forbidden, or a legitimate evolution. This paper presents ArchSync, a deterministic architecture-conformance prototype that treats a machine-readable model as an executable contract and reconstructs an observed service graph from TypeScript source code. Stack-specific analyzers detect selected HTTP, PostgreSQL, Redis, and AMQP interactions, preserve file-level evidence, and feed a Git-diff gate that maps introduced findings to PASS, BLOCK, or REVIEW without rewriting the approved model. We verify the prototype on two co-developed development datasets and replay the same controlled changes through the incremental gate. Within these finite regression sets, the implementation agrees with the declared graph, decision, evidence-location, and full-scan oracles, and provenance tracking removes the targeted detector errors exposed by the earlier analyzer. The study does not compare ArchSync against an external tool and does not include an independently curated real-world holdout. Its results therefore establish executable-contract coherence and reproducible regression behavior only, not comparative advantage or general accuracy on TypeScript systems.
\end{abstract}
