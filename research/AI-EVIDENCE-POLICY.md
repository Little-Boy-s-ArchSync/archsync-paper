## 1. Core rule

Every member may use an AI system to help perform or technically execute
assigned work. AI assistance is allowed for planning, drafting, translation,
coding, test generation, debugging, browser and CLI operation, authorized
database queries, source capture, artifact generation, metadata normalization,
duplicate suggestions, literature summaries, candidate screening or extraction
suggestions, formatting, validation, and quality checks.

AI output is an unverified proposal. It is never research evidence merely
because it is detailed, plausible, formatted, or generated by a capable model.
The member assigned to the task remains accountable for the final artifact,
decision, claim, and disclosure. The member does not need to type every query,
field, command, or file manually when an AI tool performs those mechanical
steps from a real authorized source.

## 2. Real-evidence requirement

Every evidence-bearing statement must be traceable to information that actually
exists and was actually observed. Depending on the task, real evidence includes:

- an official database result page and immutable export produced by a real run;
- a publication DOI, publisher URL, full text, page, section, table, or figure;
- a real repository commit, source file, patch, build log, test output, or CI run;
- a real dataset, raw result, normalized result, environment record, and hash;
- a decision personally checked and recorded by the named human reviewer; or
- an attestation and signature created by the named key holder with the real
  private key outside the repository.

An AI response, chat transcript, generated citation, invented example, test
fixture, template, or manually typed expected number is not evidence for a
research result. It may be retained as process context, but it cannot replace
the underlying source artifact.

## 3. Information AI must not invent

AI and members using AI must not fabricate or silently infer any of the
following:

- DOI, title, author, venue, publication date, indexing status, quartile,
  ranking system/year/category, quotation, page, URL, or access;
- database query execution, result count, export, timestamp, operator, or hash;
- benchmark execution, metric, denominator, timing, environment, or CI result;
- screening decision, exclusion reason, quality score, adjudication, or reviewer
  agreement attributed to a person who did not make and verify it;
- reviewer identity, GitHub approval, Ed25519 key, signature, attestation, or
  independent reproduction; or
- missing source information. Missing information remains `NR`, while a
  structurally inapplicable field uses `NA:<reason>` where the governed schema
  permits it.

If the source cannot be accessed or the fact cannot be verified, record the
blocker or missing value. Do not ask AI to fill the gap with a likely answer.

## 4. Human verification and accountability

Before AI-assisted content becomes a governed artifact, the assigned member
must:

1. inspect the original source, raw output, repository state, or official page;
2. confirm every evidence-bearing field against that source;
3. retain the exact locator, artifact path, commit, run URL, timestamp, and hash
   required by the task schema;
4. run the available deterministic validator or reproduction command;
5. correct or reject unsupported AI output; and
6. record who performed the verification and when it occurred.

AI may operate an authorized browser, database session, CLI, or local tool and
may create governed JSON, CSV, ledger, hash, test, and pull-request artifacts
from the captured output. That captured output becomes evidence only when it is
retained with provenance and checked under the normal evidence gate. A model's
statement that a command passed is not equivalent to the command output or CI
record.

## 5. AI-executed workflow

An assigned member may delegate the operational workflow to AI. This includes
opening the four approved literature databases through an authorized session,
running the exact sentinel queries, capturing official result pages and
exports, creating the six sentinel JSON files, building the canonical ledger,
running validators, preparing a pull request, and preparing the freeze bundle.

The following controls make AI-executed work valid rather than fabricated:

1. the named member authorizes the source, account/session, task, and scope;
2. the AI records the exact query or command, source URL, UTC time, result,
   retained artifact, and hash required by the governed schema;
3. the AI never substitutes a generated value for a missing source value;
4. the named reviewer inspects the final evidence bundle against the retained
   official source pages, exports, or publications, corrects any error, and
   explicitly accepts or rejects the exact commit; and
5. the human acceptance and any required signature remain attributable to the
   named human, even when AI performs the browser, file, CLI, or submission
   action after that explicit authorization.

The reviewer is not required to repeat every mechanical database query or
retype AI-created artifacts when the retained source capture and provenance are
sufficient to audit the result. AI may therefore complete the technical work;
human review is the accountability gate, not a requirement for manual data
entry.

## 6. Systematic literature review

Members may ask AI to:

- translate governed search strings into database syntax before execution;
- run sentinel and, after authorization, official queries in a real logged-in
  database session and retain official output;
- create the six sentinel JSON files, ledger, search logs, hashes, and review
  bundle from that retained output;
- normalize imported metadata and propose possible duplicate pairs;
- propose `include`, `exclude`, or `uncertain` decisions with candidate E-codes;
- propose extracted fields, summaries, quality considerations, or source
  locations; and
- calculate agreement or flow values from already frozen human records.

For the official review, AI may perform database operations but is not the
accountable database operator, reviewer, adjudicator, or evidence source. Each
named human reviewer must inspect the real record, full text, or retained
official capture, accept or correct the final decision independently, verify
the evidence location, and remain accountable for the decision. AI output does
not satisfy reviewer independence, calibration, agreement, or adjudication
until the named reviewer verifies and adopts the exact record.

Material AI assistance must be disclosed in the pull request, review note, or
governed artifact field available for that task. The disclosure identifies the
AI system or tool, the assistance scope, and the real source against which the
output was verified. Prompts and outputs are retained when required by a frozen
protocol or venue policy.

## 7. Keys, accounts, access, and security

AI must not receive private keys, access tokens, passwords, secrets, personally
identifiable information, or private source unless an approved security and
data-governance policy explicitly authorizes that provider and data flow.

AI may invoke the local key-generation or signing command after the named human
explicitly authorizes the exact path, commit, attestation, and action. The
private key must remain outside the repository and under the human key holder's
account; the AI must not read, display, copy, upload, retain, or transmit the
private-key contents. The resulting public key, signature, attestation, command
record, and commit binding may be retained as governed evidence.

AI may operate a real authorized database session and preserve the resulting
official URL, export, count, timestamp, and hash. The named human who authorized
and verifies that run remains the accountable operator recorded in the artifact.

## 8. Paper and code contributions

AI may draft paper prose, code, tests, diagrams, tables, and documentation.
Before merge:

- authors verify every citation, quotation, number, comparison, and claim;
- code owners run tests and inspect the actual output;
- the pull request identifies material AI assistance when required; and
- the venue's authorship and AI-disclosure policy is followed.

AI is not an author, reviewer, approver, or accountable contributor. Human
authors and maintainers retain responsibility for correctness, originality,
licensing, confidentiality, and research integrity.

## 9. Definition of Done

AI-assisted work is complete only when:

- the requested artifact exists in the governed repository or approved system;
- every evidence-bearing value points to a real inspectable source;
- no placeholder, mock result, invented citation, or inferred missing value is
  presented as observed evidence;
- the assigned member has verified the content and recorded provenance;
- any AI-executed browser, database, CLI, artifact, approval-submission, or
  signing action is bound to explicit human authorization and a retained audit
  record;
- required independent review remains independent and human-accountable; and
- applicable validators, tests, CI, and reproduction gates pass.
